## 4. Public profiles and leaderboards

Your profile is private until you make it public. A public profile shows your display name and every game you uploaded, in full, to anyone, and anyone can link to it or copy what it shows. You can make it private again at any time; copies others already made are out of our control.

With a public profile, a display name and enough games, you also appear on the public leaderboards, which rank players on their uploaded games. The privacy policy says what they show and how to leave them (turn the profile private: the boards stop showing you as soon as you save). Leaderboards are for fun: the numbers are self-reported by each player's app, there are no prizes, and we do not promise they are accurate or complete. If games look changed to climb a board, we may take the steps in section 3, such as leaving them off the boards.

If you turn on "Include my games in community hero stats" on your account page (it is off until you do), your games count toward the community hero stats: totals per hero across many players, which never name you. The privacy policy says what is used and how to stop. Like the leaderboards, these numbers are self-reported, for information and fun, and we do not promise they are accurate or complete; games that look changed may be left out.

## 9. The game

Tavern Ledger reads the log files the game writes on your PC. By default (you can turn it off at any time in Settings, under "Read from the game", and the app keeps your choice; if you never chose, including on an install from before 16 October 2026, it is on), the app also reads two values the game shows you, the lobby's five tribes and your own Battlegrounds rating, from the game's memory while it runs, with read-only access. The values stay on your PC and are never uploaded. Reading the game's memory goes against the letter of Blizzard's End User License Agreement (section 1.C); Blizzard has tolerated such reads by other trackers for years, but it has not approved this one and could act against it at any time. The risk to your Battle.net account is yours, and it applies for as long as this is on: turn it off in Settings if you do not want to take it. Tavern Ledger never injects code or acts in the game for you, and it changes no game file except one thing: only when you click Fix in the app's setup notice, it can create or complete Hearthstone's two logging settings files, `log.config` and `client.config`, keeping a `.bak` copy of any file it changes. Your use of Hearthstone stays subject to Blizzard's own terms.
