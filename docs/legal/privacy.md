# Privacy policy

Last updated: 27 October 2026

Tavern Ledger is a free, open-source Hearthstone Battlegrounds tracker: a Windows app that reads the game's log files on your PC, and a website where you can keep and, if you want, share your games. This policy says what personal data we handle, why, where it goes and what you can do about it.

Unofficial fan project. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Blizzard Entertainment.

## 2. The desktop app keeps your data on your PC

The app reads the game's log files and saves a summary of each Battlegrounds game in `%APPDATA%\TavernLedger\games.jsonl`: the session's start time, mode, game build, your hero and your Duos teammate's hero, the lobby's heroes by player number, places and health, boards (as card ids) with each side's hero powers and trinkets (card ids and the numbers their text shows), card names in your game's language, tribes offered in the tavern, your own shop each turn (the cards offered, bought and sold, rolls, freezes, gold and tavern tier) and the actions the game recorded you making, each with the time since the game started (to work out your actions per minute; together with the game's start time, this shows roughly when each action happened), and which parser version read the game. The logs contain the names (BattleTags) of the players in your lobby; the app does not save them.

**Reading from the game (on by default; you can turn it off).** Unless you turn off "Read the lobby's tribes and your rating from the game" in Settings, the app also reads, read-only, two values the game shows you while it runs: the five tribes of the lobby you are in and your own Battlegrounds rating (Solo and Duos). It reads nothing else from the game: no other players' names, BattleTags, account ids or ratings. These values stay on your PC: the app keeps them in its own files in its data folder, never puts them in the game record or a problem report, and never uploads them. Turning the switch off forgets them and deletes those files. If you chose before, your choice stays; if you never chose (a new install, or an older one that never touched the switch), it is on. Turning the switch off keeps only the setting itself (`game_memory.json`). Reading the game's memory goes against the letter of Blizzard's End User License Agreement (section 1.C), and the account at risk is yours: the terms (section 9) say so in full.

Unless you turn on uploading, we never receive this file, and the app sends nothing about you or your games over the internet: its only other requests are for card data, minion pictures and updates (below). To export it, copy it; to delete it, delete the `TavernLedger` folder, or tick "Also delete my games and settings" when you uninstall the app.

**Diagnostics you copy.** In Settings, "Copy diagnostics" shows you, then copies to your clipboard, a short text: the app version, the game build, the parser version, the Power settings of your `log.config` and a few known error messages of the app. It has no names, accounts, paths or keys. The app never sends it; if you paste it into a message, you chose to.

**Uploading to your account (optional, off by default).** You can sign in from the app with a one-time link sent to your email: the app opens our website's sign-in page in your browser, where you ask for the link (with the check in section 3), and you open the link on the same PC; the app never asks for your password. It keeps the sign-in (a refresh token) in Windows Credential Manager on your PC, and a list of the games it uploaded next to the history (`uploads.jsonl`). Only when you turn "Upload games" on does it send each finished game's record (the summary above, without player names) to your account (section 4). Signing out ends the session on our server, removes the sign-in from your PC and turns uploading off.

**Card names and pictures.** The app downloads card data (names and card text) and card pictures from HearthstoneJSON (`api.hearthstonejson.com` and `art.hearthstonejson.com`, run by HearthSim, section 5) and keeps them on your PC: it asks for the card data again when its copy is a week old or a game shows a card it does not know, and for a picture only when it is about to show a card it has no picture of. Each request tells HearthSim your PC's IP address, the app's user agent (`TavernLedger`, its version and the website's address) and the file asked for, which for a picture names the card. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in showing your games in a readable way (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

**Minion pictures.** When it has no complete set of them (the first time it runs, or after an update that brings a newer set), the app downloads the images that draw a minion like the game (its frame, attack and health gems and keyword marks; a few small files, 2 MB at most) from our image host, `assets.tavernledger.net`, run by us on Cloudflare (section 5), and keeps them on your PC. If a download fails it tries again at most every 15 minutes. Each request tells that host your PC's IP address and the app's user agent (`TavernLedger` and its version), like any web request, and the address of the file asked for, which is the same for every user; nothing else is sent. Cloudflare keeps the request logs as in section 3. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in showing your games in a readable way (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

**Updates.** From version 0.2.0, the app asks the project's releases on GitHub whether a new version is out, a minute after it starts and every six hours, unless you turn off "Check for updates automatically" in Settings > Updates; "Check now" there asks once. When there is a new version, it downloads the installer from the same place and keeps it only if it is signed by the project's key; it installs only when you press "Restart now". Each request tells GitHub your PC's IP address and the app's updater user agent (`tauri-plugin-updater` and its version), and nothing about you or your games. GitHub decides what it logs; see GitHub's privacy statement. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and secure (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

## 3. When you visit the website

When you load a page, our hosting provider (Cloudflare) and, for pages that talk to our server, our backend provider (Supabase) receive your IP address, browser user agent, the address requested and the time, as with any website. They use it to deliver the page and to protect the service, and keep it in their logs (section 6).

**The check that you are not a robot.** The forms that sign you in, create an account, send a password reset or sign-in link, or check your password before your account is deleted show Cloudflare Turnstile, which checks that a person is using the page. It runs in a frame from Cloudflare (`challenges.cloudflare.com`), which receives your IP address, browser user agent, the TLS details of your connection and the page's address, and may ask you to tick a box. We receive only a one-time pass, which our backend checks with Cloudflare before it sends an email or checks a password. We use it to keep robots from creating accounts, sending our emails to people who did not ask for them and guessing passwords (legitimate interest in keeping the service and accounts safe, Art. 6(1)(f)). Cloudflare does this as our processor (section 5); under its Turnstile privacy addendum it also uses these signals, as a controller, to improve its bot detection. Pages without these forms do not load it.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in running a website that works and is protected from attacks (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

**Card pictures.** Pages that show games (a game, your games, a public profile, the games list and the leaderboards) load card and hero pictures straight from HearthstoneJSON's image server (`art.hearthstonejson.com`), an independent service run by HearthSim, not a provider of ours. Your browser sends it your IP address, browser user agent and the address of each picture, which names only the card: our pages send no referrer, so it does not learn which page, account or game you are looking at. HearthSim decides what it logs; see hearthstonejson.com. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in showing games in a readable way (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). To avoid it, block that address in your browser: the pages then show a plain placeholder instead.

**Minion pictures.** The same pages also load the images that draw a minion like the game (its frame, attack and health gems and keyword marks) from a separate image site of ours, `assets.tavernledger.net`, run by us on Cloudflare (section 5), with no cookies. Your browser sends it your IP address and browser user agent and the address of each image, which is the same for every visitor; our pages send no referrer, so it does not learn which page, account or game you are looking at. Cloudflare keeps the request logs as in section 3. Legal basis: our legitimate interest in showing games in a readable way (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). To avoid it, block that address in your browser: the pages then draw minions in their plain style.

There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking.

### Public profiles and leaderboards

**Public profile.** If you turn it on, anyone on the internet can see your display name and read every game you uploaded (in full while its full record is kept, section 6, and its summary after that), and can link to or copy them. Turning it off stops access through our site; copies others already made are out of our reach. The profile page asks search engines not to index it, but that is a request, not protection: anyone with the address can open it. Visitors see each game's summary and the day it was played. A game's address and the name of its file are a random code with no time in them, and the database does not give visitors the session's start time. The file storage does tell visitors when each file was stored on our server, which is usually soon after the game. A game's full record, while we keep it (section 6), is still readable by anyone and still holds that start time and each action's time since the game began (section 2). Visitors do not see when a game's record was saved or updated, its file hash or size, whether its full record has been deleted (only you see that), or when you created the account or changed the profile. Your display name must use Latin letters (accents are fine), digits, spaces and `_ . -` only, and no other public profile can have the same name.

**Leaderboards** (`/leaderboards/`). While your profile is public and has a display name, you can also appear on the public leaderboards, which rank players on their uploaded games, per mode (Solo, Duos) and period (the last 7 days, the last 30 days, all time). A board shows your display name with a link to your public profile, your place on the board, the number it ranks (for example your average place, top 4 or top 2 rate, win rate, games played, longest top-half streak, average place with a hero and that hero, actions per minute, gold beyond income, rolls or triples per game, your biggest board or most golden minions) and how many games it is based on. You appear on a board once you have one finished game in that mode and period that has the number (one with the hero for the per-hero board); a number from fewer than 20 games (10 with the hero) is marked "few games". The numbers are self-reported: they come from the games your app uploaded; our server checks each game's record and leaves out games with values no real game can have, but cannot prove a game was played as uploaded. The leaderboards never show your email, BattleTag, rating or other players' names. The page asks search engines not to index it.

**Leaving the leaderboards.** Turn "Public profile" off on your account page, or remove your display name: from the moment you save, our server stops showing you on every board, and the stored copy of your leaderboard numbers is deleted at the same time (or, if a rebuild is running just then, by that rebuild or the next one). A leaderboard page someone already has open keeps what it loaded until they reload it. If you make your profile public again, you come back at the next rebuild; rebuilds are scheduled every 10 minutes.

**How long the numbers are kept.** The boards are rebuilt from the current games every 10 minutes; each rebuild replaces the last one, so no history of past boards is kept. Your numbers go when you make your profile private, remove your display name or delete your account, and from backups as in section 6.

**Display names.** When you set a display name, our server checks it against a short list of offensive words and names that would look official (such as "admin" or "Blizzard") and refuses it if it matches; the account page then asks you for another one. A name set before a word was added to the list is hidden from the leaderboards until you change it. The check only compares words; it decides nothing else about you.

### Community hero stats

**What it is.** The hero stats page (`/heroes/`) shows, per mode (Solo, Duos) and period (the last 30 days, all time), how each hero does: roughly how many games and players are behind it, how often it is picked when offered, its average place, top 4 (Solo) or top 2 (Duos) rate and win rate.

**Whose games.** Only the games of accounts that turned on "Include my games in community hero stats" on their account page. The switch is off for every account, including accounts made before this page existed, and no update turns it on. It is separate from your public profile: a public profile is not counted unless you turn the switch on, and a private profile can be. We keep whether the switch is on, when you last changed it and which version of this policy was in force then.

**What is used.** From each of your finished uploaded games that our server has summarized, up to the day before each rebuild: the mode, the day it was played, your hero, your final place and, if the app recorded your hero choice, the heroes you were offered. Games with values no real game can have are left out, as on the leaderboards. To count distinct players and apply the rules below, the rebuild uses your account id internally; it is not stored with the totals.

**What is shown.** Only totals per hero, and only for a hero with at least 50 games from at least 5 players whose accounts are at least 7 days old (games of newer accounts are left out of every number), where no one player has more than half of the games and no two players more than three quarters. The pick rate follows the same rule over the games that offered the hero. All time shows a hero only if its games from before the last 30 days pass the same rule on their own (or there are none), so the two periods cannot be subtracted to single out a few games. Counts are shown in tens of games and fives of players ("50+", "5+"), average place to one decimal and rates in whole percent. No display name, account, BattleTag, rating or single game is shown, and the stored totals keep no account id. The page asks search engines not to index it.

**What remains possible.** The totals are rebuilt once a day from whole days of games, so each change mixes everyone's games of that day. Still, with few players, someone who saves the page every day and already knows something about you (for example that you have just started or stopped sharing, or the games on your public profile) could make a rough guess about your results or about whether you share; the rounding keeps it to a guess, not an exact result. Anyone can save what the page shows; we keep no history of past totals ourselves.

**Stopping.** Turn the switch off on your account page. Your games are left out from the next daily rebuild. If a rebuild ever fails, the page shows no numbers once the last one is more than 26 hours old, so your games never stay in what is shown for longer than that. Deleting your account works the same way. A page someone already has open keeps what it loaded until they reload it. Withdrawing your consent does not change what was shown before.

### Improving the simulator

**What it is.** The combat simulator (the odds on the website's simulator page and in the app's overlay) is checked against what really happened in games, to find the card rules it gets wrong. Until now it was checked only on the project owner's own games. If you turn on "Let my games be used to improve the simulator" on your account page, yours can be used too.

**Whose games.** Only the games of accounts that turned the switch on. It is off for every account, including accounts made before it existed, and no update turns it on. It is separate from your public profile and from the community hero stats: neither implies it. We keep whether the switch is on, when you last turned it on or off and which version of this policy was in force then.

**What is used.** The full records of your uploaded games that our server still keeps (section 6), including games uploaded before you turned the switch on: the boards at each combat, yours and your opponents' (card ids, Attack, Health, keywords), and the combat replays (each attack and what it did), with the game's mode and build. The records hold no player names, BattleTags or ratings (section 2). Game summaries, your profile and your account details are not used.

**How.** From time to time the project's owner lists the full records of the accounts whose switch is on at that moment, downloads them to their own computer, removes the account id from each file name, and runs the project's own open-source measuring tool on them. The tool counts how often the simulator's odds matched the real result and lists the combats it got wrong with their cause, so the card rule behind them can be found and fixed. The downloaded copies are deleted from that computer when the run ends, the same day. What is kept: totals over all the games measured (counts, shares, causes, card ids) and the fixed rules in the code. Totals may be published (for example on the accuracy page or in the project's repository), only as totals over all the games measured: never per player, never with a display name, account id, BattleTag or date, and never a single game. A test added to the code with a fix uses made-up boards, never a copy of your game.

**Stopping.** Turn the switch off on your account page. From that moment your games are no longer listed, so no later measurement uses them. A run already under way when you turn it off finishes with the copies it has, and they are deleted the same day. Totals already worked out and rules already fixed stay, because they cannot be traced back to you or to your games. Deleting your account works the same way. Withdrawing your consent does not change what was measured before.

### Training the expected-place model

**What it is.** The expected place is a number for each turn of a game: the place, from 1 to 8 in Solo or 1 to 4 in Duos, that a game in that position usually ends in, with how sure the number is. The app's recap and the website's game page show it beside your health, on your own games. It comes from a small model trained on real games: it learns how games in a given position ended.

**Whose games.** The uploaded games of every account that has not turned "Use my uploaded games to train the expected-place model" off on its account page. The switch is on for every account, including accounts made before it existed, because the legal basis is our legitimate interest, not consent; turning it off is your right to object, and you never need to say why. Uploading itself stays off until you turn it on, so no game leaves your PC for this unless you upload it. The switch is separate from the simulator switch, the community hero stats and your public profile: none of them changes another.

**What is used.** The full records of your uploaded games that our server still keeps (section 6): for each turn, your hero, health, tavern tier, gold, board and the tribes in the lobby when known, the boards of the opponents you met, and your final place, with the game's mode and build. The records hold no player names, BattleTags or ratings (section 2). Game summaries, your profile and your account details are not used.

**How.** From time to time the project's owner lists the full records of the accounts whose switch is on at that moment, downloads them to their own computer, removes the account id from each file name and runs the project's own open-source training tool on them. The model never takes an account id as input. The downloaded copies are deleted from that computer when the run ends, the same day. What is kept: the model's weights and totals over all the games used. The weights are published under the project's licence with a description of what they were trained on (how many games and accounts, rounded, which builds, how well calibrated); they are published only once enough games from enough accounts were used that no single account shapes them. Nothing is kept or published per player, with a display name, account id, BattleTag or date, or for a single game. The model then runs on each player's own PC and on the website for their own games; it takes no decision about you.

**Objecting.** Turn the switch off on your account page. From that moment your games are no longer listed, so no later training run uses them. A run already under way when you turn it off finishes with the copies it has, and they are deleted the same day. A model already trained and totals already worked out stay, because they cannot be traced back to you or to your games. Deleting your account works the same way. Turning the switch off never removes a feature: you still see the expected place on your games.

## 6. How long we keep it

- **Account, profile, game summaries and sessions:** until you delete your account (sessions also end when you sign out or they expire; each account keeps at most 10, the most recently used, and the clean-up job deletes older ones every day).
- **Full game records** (each game's turn-by-turn boards, shops and combat replays, as uploaded by the app): 28 days from the day the game was played (the date on your PC, or the day we first received the game if that is earlier), then deleted (we wait one more day to allow for time zones). Deletion runs in daily batches after that date and can take longer: some days when this rule starts and older records are cleared, and a game uploaded again in the last hour waits for a later day. A game whose summary our server has not been able to make keeps its full record until it has one, since the record is the only way to make it. Copies in backups expire after 35 days (below). The game's summary stays, so your games list, stats and leaderboard numbers do not change, and the game's page then shows that summary with a line saying its full details have expired. The app keeps every game in full on your PC (section 2). If the app uploads a new copy of an old game, that copy is deleted by a later clean-up, under the same conditions and with the same possible delays. Accounts with unlimited history (section 4) keep their full records until the account is deleted.
- **Leaderboard numbers:** rebuilt every 10 minutes while your profile is public; deleted when you make it private or delete your account.
- **Community stats choice:** until you delete your account. **Community hero stats:** rebuilt once a day; your games leave them at the first rebuild after you turn the switch off or delete your account, and the page shows nothing once the last rebuild is over 26 hours old.
- **Simulator choice:** until you delete your account. **Use of your games for the simulator:** only while the switch is on, and only the full records still kept (28 days for accounts without unlimited history, as above); the owner's downloaded copies are deleted the same day as each run. The totals keep no account id and are kept as long as they are useful for the simulator.
- **Training choice:** until you delete your account. **Use of your games for training:** only while the switch is on, and only the full records still kept (28 days for accounts without unlimited history, as above); the owner's downloaded copies are deleted the same day as each run. The model's weights and the totals keep no account id and are kept as long as the model is in use.
- **Security log:** 30 days, then deleted every day by our clean-up job; all of it goes earlier if you delete your account.
- **Backups:** made weekly on the controller's own computer, readable only by their user account, and deleted after 35 days. After you delete your account, your data can remain in backups for up to 35 days. If we ever restore a backup, we delete again the accounts deleted since it was made.
- **Upload times:** two days. **Refused-request counters:** one hour.
- **Provider logs** (page requests, server requests, emails sent): kept by Cloudflare, Supabase and the email sender for the periods their services set, then deleted. They are not tied to your account in a way we can export or delete one by one. Supabase keeps its API, database and sign-in logs for one day on our plan. Lettermint keeps each email it sent, with its delivery record, for 28 days and then deletes it. Cloudflare does not give us the website's request logs on our plan and does not publish a fixed period for them; under its privacy policy it keeps them only as long as it needs to run and protect its network. For mail you send to our contact addresses, Cloudflare keeps a delivery record (sender, recipient, subject, time and result) for 31 days; the message itself is passed on to our mailbox.

## 8. Cookies and browser storage

The website sets no cookies of its own and uses no tracking. When you sign in, the Supabase library stores your sign-in session in your browser's local storage (key `sb-<project>-auth-token`: access and refresh tokens and your account's basic details) and, while you sign up or reset your password, a one-time code verifier (`…-code-verifier`) that proves the email link came back to the browser that asked for it. These are strictly necessary: the session is removed when you sign out, and a code verifier when its link is used or a newer one replaces it. Our hosting provider, Cloudflare, sets a cookie only if its protection against attacks shows you a challenge: `cf_clearance`, which records that you passed it so you are not asked again for a while. It lasts 30 minutes (our zone's setting), is strictly necessary and is not used to track you. Cloudflare's bot cookie `__cf_bm` is not used on this site (its bot features are off), and Cloudflare's Network Error Logging (browser reports of failed connections) is turned off. The check that you are not a robot (section 3) runs in Cloudflare's own frame and sets no cookie on our site.

When accounts are open, the website also installs a small program in your browser (a service worker) so the site can be added to your home screen or app list and its public pages open without a connection. It keeps copies of the site's public pages, styles, scripts, fonts and icons in your browser's cache storage (names starting `tl-v-`). These copies are the same for every visitor, stay on your device and are never sent to us. The program never keeps account, sign-in or dashboard pages, anything with a query in its address, or anything from our account service. Clearing the site's data in your browser removes the copies and the program.

If you turn on "Keep my last games on this device" on your games list (off by default), the website saves a copy of each of your own games that you open, up to the last 10, in your browser's cache storage (a cache named `tl-replays`) on this device, together with a copy of the game page, so you can watch them without a connection. These copies are the same files your browser downloads to show the game, never leave your device and are not read by us. They are deleted when you sign out, when you turn the setting off, when you press "Delete saved games", or when you clear the site's data in your browser. Games of other players are never saved.
